## Purpose

The DevOS Constitution is the set of binding standards that constrain product, engineering,
and AI decisions across DevOS. Where a Constitution article and any other document conflict,
the Constitution governs.

It exists because a growing organisation cannot rely on shared intuition. Standards that are
not written down are renegotiated in every review, and drift toward whatever is expedient.

## What the Constitution binds

| Bound | Not bound |
| --- | --- |
| DevOS platform product decisions | A customer's choices for their own system |
| DevOS engineering practice | Third-party tool internals |
| DevOS AI agent behaviour | The output quality of a model we do not control |
| This documentation repository | Marketing and commercial material |
| Framework and agent authoring | Individual writing style within the content standard |

The Constitution constrains what DevOS does. It does not constrain what DevOS users choose
to build — DevOS recommends and records; it does not impose architecture on customers.

## How to read it

Articles use RFC 2119 requirement language:

| Keyword | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MUST** / **MUST NOT** | Absolute. A violation is a defect and blocks release. |
| **SHOULD** / **SHOULD NOT** | Strong recommendation. Deviation requires a recorded reason in the relevant decision record. |
| **MAY** | Genuinely optional. |

A **SHOULD** deviation is legitimate when documented. An undocumented deviation is a defect
regardless of how reasonable it was.

## Precedence

When documents conflict, this order applies:

1. The Constitution
2. Accepted RFCs
3. Engine, product, and architecture specifications
4. Reference material and templates
5. Guidance pages and examples

A specification that contradicts the Constitution is wrong and must be corrected, not
reconciled. If the Constitution is the thing that is wrong, amend it through the process in
[Versioning](/constitution/versioning) — do not work around it.

## Compliance

Compliance is checked at three points:

| Point | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Design review | Does the proposed behaviour satisfy the applicable articles? |
| Pull request review | Does the implementation or document violate a MUST? |
| Release review | Are all outstanding SHOULD deviations recorded with reasons? |

A reviewer may block a change by citing an article. The response is either to comply, to
record a justified deviation where the article permits one, or to open an amendment RFC.
Arguing that an article is inconvenient is not a response.

## Amendment

The Constitution is amended only through the RFC process, with the review requirements set
out in [Governance](/constitution/governance) and the versioning rules in
[Versioning](/constitution/versioning). Amendments are never made silently; every change is
recorded in the version history of the affected article.

## Status

**Status: Production.** The Constitution is in force. Unlike capability documentation, its
status refers to the governance instrument itself, not to any implementation.

Articles that constrain unbuilt capabilities are still binding on the design of those
capabilities.
