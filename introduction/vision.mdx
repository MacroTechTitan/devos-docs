## Purpose

This page states what DevOS is aiming at over a multi-year horizon, and the conditions
under which we would consider the product a success or a failure. It is a statement of
intent, not a description of shipped capability.

## The vision

**Every software project should begin with a documented, reviewable, defensible design —
and producing that design should take hours, not weeks.**

Today, the gap between an idea and a sound technical plan is bridged by whoever happens to
be senior enough to hold the whole picture in their head. That approach does not scale, it
does not survive staff turnover, and it produces plans that cannot be audited because they
were never written down.

DevOS aims to make the design phase a repeatable, inspectable process that produces
artefacts a stranger could pick up and act on.

## What we are building toward

### Design intent becomes a durable artefact

The product brief stops being a conversation and becomes a structured document that every
downstream stage reads. When an implementer asks "why is it built this way?", the answer is
a link, not a memory.

### Recommendations carry honest uncertainty

Architecture advice is delivered with a confidence score and named evidence gaps. A system
that says "0.42 confidence, because we do not know your expected concurrency" is more
useful than one that answers with unearned assurance. Calibration matters more than
apparent competence.

### Decisions are immutable and superseding

The Decision Ledger records what was decided, when, on what evidence, and what was rejected.
Records are superseded rather than edited, so the reasoning history survives. A project's
architecture becomes legible over time rather than archaeological.

### AI build work is constrained by design, not by prompt craft

AI coding tools produce coherent output when they are given boundaries: the decisions that
apply, the interfaces they must honour, the scope they must not exceed. DevOS aims to
generate those boundaries automatically, so quality does not depend on how well an
individual writes prompts.

### The framework library encodes real experience

Reference frameworks capture what has actually worked for a class of system — including the
constraints that make a pattern inappropriate. A framework that only lists its strengths is
marketing; a framework that names its failure modes is engineering.

## What success looks like

We will judge DevOS against outcomes, not usage:

| Outcome | Signal |
| --- | --- |
| Design work is faster | Time from idea to accepted architecture decisions falls materially against the team's own prior baseline. |
| Decisions survive | A project's ledger still explains its architecture accurately six months after the decisions were made. |
| Confidence is calibrated | High-confidence recommendations are reversed less often than low-confidence ones, measured on real projects. |
| Blueprints are actionable | Implementers begin work from the blueprint without needing a separate verbal briefing. |
| Rework falls | Fewer architecture reversals attributable to a constraint that was known but never recorded. |

We have not yet measured any of these. Establishing the baselines is part of the work; see
[Roadmap](/introduction/roadmap).

## What would mean we failed

Stating the failure conditions is part of the vision, because a vision that cannot be
falsified is a slogan.

- **Confidence scores that are not calibrated.** If a 0.9 score is wrong as often as a 0.5
  score, the scoring is theatre and should be removed rather than tuned.
- **Ledgers nobody reads.** If decision records are written and never consulted, DevOS has
  added ceremony rather than clarity.
- **Blueprints that need translation.** If every blueprint requires a senior engineer to
  reinterpret it before work can start, the artefact has failed at its only job.
- **Recommendations that regress to a default.** If the Decision Engine effectively
  recommends the same stack regardless of brief, the framework matching is decorative.
- **Documentation that drifts from behaviour.** If pages describe capabilities that do not
  work as written, the knowledge base becomes a liability — for humans and for the agents
  that retrieve it.

## Boundaries of the vision

DevOS deliberately does not aim to:

- Replace engineering judgement. Every ledger entry requires human acceptance.
- Write and own the application code.
- Become a project management or issue-tracking system.
- Guarantee outcomes. A well-documented design can still be the wrong design; DevOS makes
  that visible earlier, not impossible.
