## Purpose

This page defines the user types DevOS designs for. Personas exist to settle design
arguments: a proposal that serves no persona's stated need is not a product improvement.

These are role archetypes derived from the problem DevOS addresses, not profiles of named
individuals or research participants.

## Persona summary

| Persona | Primary need | Workspace | Typical role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical founder | Make defensible architecture choices without a senior team | Customer | Owner |
| Engineering lead | Stop re-explaining decisions and losing their reasoning | Customer | Contributor or Admin |
| Solo consultant | Produce design documentation clients can act on | Customer | Owner |
| Implementing engineer | Understand why the system is built this way | Customer | Viewer |
| Platform administrator | Control access and maintain the framework library | Admin | Admin |
| Auditor or reviewer | Verify that decisions were made and recorded properly | Customer | Viewer |

---

## Technical founder

**Situation.** Building a product with a small team, or alone. Has enough technical judgement
to know that the early architecture choices matter, and not enough breadth to be confident
across all of them.

**Needs**

- Candidate architectures with honest confidence, not a single confident answer.
- To know which of their unknowns actually matters — most do not.
- A record they can hand to the first engineer they hire.

**What they do**

Run the interview themselves. Spend real time on the confidence review, because the evidence
gaps tell them what to go and find out. Accept decisions personally.

**Design implications**

- Evidence gaps must be phrased so a non-specialist can act on them. "Expected concurrency
  unknown" is useful; a purely technical restatement is not.
- Recommendations must include the operational cost of running the architecture, since this
  persona will be the one operating it.
- Low confidence must not read as product failure. The gap is the deliverable.

## Engineering lead

**Situation.** Responsible for a system built by several people over time. Spends
disproportionate effort re-explaining choices made months ago, and re-litigating decisions
whose original constraints have been forgotten.

**Needs**

- Decision records that survive staff turnover and still explain the architecture accurately.
- Rejected alternatives preserved with their reasons, so old arguments do not restart.
- A way to change a decision without erasing the history of the previous one.

**What they do**

Use the ledger as the reference point in design discussions. Supersede decisions when
constraints change. Point new joiners at the ledger rather than repeating themselves.

**Design implications**

- Supersession must be a first-class operation, not a workaround.
- The ledger must be readable as a narrative, not only queryable as records.
- Attribution must survive a member leaving the organisation — see
  [Roles and workspaces](/getting-started/roles-and-workspaces).

## Solo consultant

**Situation.** Designs systems for clients who will build them with someone else. The design
document is the deliverable.

**Needs**

- Output that a third party can implement without a verbal briefing.
- Visible reasoning, because the reasoning is what the client is paying for.
- Reusable structure across engagements without reusing conclusions.

**What they do**

Run many short projects. Rely on the blueprint as a client-facing artefact. Care about the
framework library because it encodes reusable judgement.

**Design implications**

- Blueprints must stand alone. A blueprint requiring the author present has failed.
- Framework reuse must not leak one client's context into another's project — Article 7 in
  [Principles](/constitution/principles).
- Export matters more for this persona than for any other.

## Implementing engineer

**Situation.** Building from someone else's design. Did not attend the decisions and cannot
ask about half of them.

**Needs**

- To know which decisions constrain the code they are writing right now.
- To understand why, so they do not accidentally undo a choice made for a reason they cannot see.
- Scoped work with an unambiguous definition of done.

**What they do**

Read the ledger and blueprint. Work through build units. Rarely accept decisions — usually a
Viewer.

**Design implications**

- Build units must carry the applicable decisions inline, not by reference alone.
- Traceability must run downward as well as upward: from a module to the decisions that shaped
  it — PS-8 in [Product standards](/constitution/product-standards).
- Viewer must be genuinely useful, not a degraded experience.

## Platform administrator

**Situation.** Responsible for DevOS itself inside an organisation. Manages who has access
and maintains the framework library.

**Needs**

- Clear role semantics with predictable blast radius.
- An audit trail of who did what.
- Framework authoring that enforces the framework standard rather than trusting authors.

**What they do**

Work primarily in the admin workspace. Review the audit log. Adapt frameworks to house
constraints.

**Design implications**

- Administration must be separated from project work, so neither is one misclick from the
  other.
- Framework authoring must enforce Article 6 — a framework without named failure modes cannot
  be published.
- Role changes must be immediate and audited.

---

## Auditor or reviewer

**Situation.** Verifying that a design process happened properly — internal review, due
diligence, or regulatory examination.

**Needs**

- Evidence that decisions were made deliberately, by identified people, on stated evidence.
- Complete supersession chains, including reversed decisions.
- Read access that cannot alter anything.

**What they do**

Read ledgers and audit logs. Trace decisions to briefs and blueprints to decisions. Change
nothing.

**Design implications**

- Superseded records must remain retrievable with their original content intact.
- Read-only must be enforced structurally, not by interface omission.
- Time-bounded external access is an open question — see
  [Roles and workspaces](/getting-started/roles-and-workspaces).

## Who DevOS is not for

Stating this prevents design drift toward users the product cannot serve well.

| Not a persona | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A non-technical user expecting DevOS to make the choices | Every decision requires human acceptance. Someone must exercise judgement. |
| A team wanting code generated end to end | DevOS produces specifications and prompts, not applications. |
| A project manager tracking delivery | DevOS produces build units; it is not a tracker. |
| A large enterprise needing self-hosted or air-gapped operation | Deferred — see [Roadmap](/introduction/roadmap). |
| Someone with a finished specification wanting validation | The interview tests assumptions; it does not ratify them. |

## Persona conflicts

Where two personas want opposite things, these resolutions apply:

| Conflict | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Founder wants a single clear answer; PS-4 requires plural recommendations | Plural recommendations, ranked by scored evidence only. Clarity comes from the evidence, not from suppressing options. |
| Consultant wants blueprint export; administrator wants data control | Export is permitted within the organisation boundary and audited. Cross-organisation export is not a permission that exists. |
| Implementing engineer wants terse build units; auditor wants full context | Build units carry applicable decisions inline; full context remains one link away. |
| Lead wants to correct a wrong decision; auditor needs history intact | Supersession. The record is never edited. |
